# How to compare the real cost of KYC per verification

Price lists are not comparable until you know what each vendor counts as **one verification**, and what you pay when it fails.

## Questions to ask each vendor
1. **What is one unit?** One ID check, one applicant, one session, or one bundle (ID + selfie + liveness)?
2. **Are failures and retries billed?** Some vendors bill only successful checks; others bill every completed check.
3. **Is there a monthly minimum or platform fee?**
4. **Which parts are add-ons?** Sanctions/PEP screening, ongoing monitoring, longer retention, extra data fields, human review.
5. **What does a free tier or trial include?**
6. **Are prices public?** Prices can change; keep the date you read them.

## The formula
Cost per approved customer =

(monthly fee + units billed × price per unit + add-ons) ÷ approved customers

Your units billed are higher than your approved customers because of rejections, retries and drop-off. Measure your own rates from a pilot; do not use a vendor's.

## An illustrative example (made-up numbers)
Suppose 1,000 applicants start. 900 complete and 800 are approved. A vendor bills every completed check at $0.50 with a $100 monthly minimum, and screening at $0.20 per check. Billed: 900 × (0.50 + 0.20) = $630, which is above the minimum, so $630 ÷ 800 = about $0.79 per approved customer. A vendor that bills only approved checks at $0.60 plus the same screening would cost 800 × 0.80 = $640, or $0.80 per approved customer. The lists looked $0.10 apart; the real gap is about one cent. Your own approval and retry rates decide which is cheaper.

## How Armith counts
A verification is counted once, when its ID check completes, whatever the outcome. There is no monthly minimum. See [Pricing and billing](/pricing) and the usage CSV, which separates decided cases from the rest.
